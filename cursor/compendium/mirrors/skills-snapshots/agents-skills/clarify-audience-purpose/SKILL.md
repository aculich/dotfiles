# Clarify audience and purpose

Anchor envisioning in **who** and **why** before **what**.

## When to Use

- “Users” or “the business” appear without personas or owners.
- Success is defined as delivery (“we shipped”) not outcome (“behavior changed”).
- Multiple departments read the same doc with different risks.

## Workflow

1. **Primary audience** — who must love the outcome for the bet to be worth it?
2. **Secondary / anti-audiences** — who might veto, misuse, or be harmed?
3. **Job statement** — “When \<situation\>, they want \<progress\>, so they can \<outcome\>” (adapt JTBD-style wording; validate with the user).
4. **Success signals** — observable within an agreed horizon; include at least one **lag** and one **lead** indicator if possible.
5. **Anti-goals** — explicit non-goals to prevent scope soup.
6. **Owner** — who can accept tradeoffs on the user’s behalf?
7. **Next skill**: proceed to [survey-creative-landscape](../survey-creative-landscape/SKILL.md) unless the task is purely narrative foresight—then jump to [envision-end-state](../envision-end-state/SKILL.md).

## Principles

- Ask **high return-on-questions**; one precise question beats five vague ones.
- Treat marketplace hype as weak evidence; prefer the user’s lived examples.
- If the audience lattice cannot be filled, say so and pause rather than inventing personas.
