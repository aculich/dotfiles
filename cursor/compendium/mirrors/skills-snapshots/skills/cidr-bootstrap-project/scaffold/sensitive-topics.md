# Sensitive Topics & Framing Guidance

Things to be careful about when drafting communications or documents for this project. Adapt to local political context.

## DO NOT

- **Do not request or mention defendant names** in court communications. Privacy concern is real — name + address allows looking up individual case details.
- **Do not frame this as a county or advocacy request** to the court. Frame it as **UC Berkeley / CiDR Lab academic research**.
- **Do not lead with "we will file a motion"** in initial court communications. Informal / county-facilitated approach first; a research-access motion is **long-lead** (~6 months minimum), not a credible near-term threat.
- **Court data access — use a light touch**: politics around bulk court data vary by county and state; pushing too hard early can close doors. Prefer cooperative, research-framed, county-chaperoned asks.
- **Do not put racial estimation language in written SOWs.** Internal analysis from public-source data is fine; the contract text should not promise racial classification of individuals.
- **Do not share individual tenant data** from any source — court records, Legal Aid, or sheriff data. Published results stay aggregated with population thresholds.
- **Do not reference confidential Legal Aid access** (e.g. statute-bound case partnership) in external communications. Frame as "advisory support from local legal services" if at all.
- **Do not underestimate data ethics under current federal landscape**. Vulnerable clients are afraid even to do intake; aggregated data still requires care.

## CAREFUL WITH

- **Bulk access to paper / imaged case files**: courts may treat large-scale file access as burdensome or sealed-record sensitive. Frame requests cooperatively; coordinate wording with counsel (e.g. Movement Legal) on precedent.
- **Racial estimation methodology**: always clarify it produces aggregate statistics, not individual classifications. BISG is established in academic literature but can be perceived as profiling if explained poorly.
- **"Crisis" language**: accurate but defensive policy audiences may push back. Lead with data, frame as "informing local policy decisions."
- **Comparisons across counties**: small absolute numbers can be misleading; emphasize rates and disparities, not raw counts.
- **Board / Council presentations**: county or city executives sometimes block findings from reaching elected bodies. Be careful about written commitments to specific Board appearances.

## GOOD FRAMING

- "UC Berkeley academic research project" — for court communications.
- "Informing local policy decisions" — for all audiences.
- "Building on the [prior CiDR/ERN study]" — continuity framing.
- "Technical assistance" — for funder framing (not "advocacy" or "investigation").
- "Sub-county / jurisdiction-level data to understand where displacement concentrates" — analytical framing.
- "Aligning reports with legislative or planning timelines" — data in service of democratic process, not partisan advocacy.
